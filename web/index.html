Terrain surface

Sand covers low ground, rock covers steep slopes, and snow settles above the snow height on gentler slopes.

Seeded color variation follows the terrain. Detail changes color, without moving the surface. Natural earth and Otherworld palettes both work with these rules.

Geological detail

Fine strata, cracks and grain appear in rendered stills. Preview shows filtered surface colors. Bump detail changes lighting, not the silhouette. Larger Feature scale makes broader patterns.

Area lights

Up to four rectangular lights, in scene units. Zero azimuth points north (−Z); negative elevation points down. Width and height shape soft shadows. RGB values describe linear emitted light. Geological surfaces use their own roughness.

Sky & atmosphere

Day 1 is January 1. 720 minutes is 12:00 UTC. Dates and coordinates stay on this device; no location permission or network lookup.

WELCOME TO LITHOSKY

Find your next horizon.

Choose a study, change its seed, then shape the mountain height and light. Everything you see is generated locally.

Use Scenes and Controls in the header to hide or reopen either side panel. Your edits and finished render stay intact. On smaller screens, choose scenes from Start with a scene.

  • Editor view switches between the saved Render camera and temporary perspective/top/front/side views. Frame selection (F) adjusts only the editor. Use this view for render saves a perspective composition. Camera bookmarks hold up to eight named snapshots; Use for render copies one into the active camera. Blank studio, Terrain canvas and Stone garden are editable authoring templates. Select primitives or groups to assign an Object material finish, tint, roughness and scale. Paint terrain materials blends rock, snow, sand and ground over terrain rules; drag and release for one Undo step, or use brush coordinates and Apply material brush. Escape cancels. Editor views and guides never enter exported pixels.
  • Objects opens a named list of terrain, water, sun/sky, camera, area lights and groups. Select in view picks the nearest visible surface or light; Escape returns to orbit. Names, visibility, locks and parent groups are undoable and saved in v11. Selecting alone does not change or save a scene. New group organizes existing objects; group visibility and locks apply to children. Unlock a parent group before editing its children. Core scene objects can be hidden; area lights and groups can be deleted. Duplicate light uses the existing area-light model. Choose a Box, Sphere, Cylinder or Cone, then Add at the aim target or Place in view. Shift/Ctrl/Command-click or Multi-select adds objects to a selection. W/E/R activate move/rotate/scale handles. Snap applies drag increments; Transform supplies numeric and keyboard adjustments. Edit objects contains group, duplicate, copy/paste, alignment and terrain placement. Escape cancels a drag and keeps redo. Primitive-only groups scale uniformly; other scene components use their existing editors.
  • Start with a scene from the menu. Try Alpine quiet, Evening highlands, or A distant world. A scene replaces the current composition; Undo brings it back.
  • Drag left/right to turn around the landscape; drag up/down to raise or lower your view. Scroll to move closer or farther away.
  • With the view focused, use arrow keys to orbit and + / − to zoom. Home resets the camera. Shift-drag or Shift-arrow keys pan the target. Escape cancels a camera drag.
  • Camera & composition saves focal length, framing, roll and focus. Enable Depth of field to trace aperture blur; Pick focus in view sets the focus plane on terrain or water. At landscape scale, blur can be subtle. Aperture does not change exposure. Render a region selects a crop without changing the camera or pixel sampling.
  • After Build with layers, choose Raise, Lower, Smooth or Flatten under Sculpt terrain. Drag on land and release to apply one stroke. Arrow keys move the brush; Space applies it; Escape cancels. Flatten uses the height where the stroke starts. Switch back to Orbit camera to turn your view.
  • Import heightmap accepts 16-bit grayscale PNG or a heightmap bundle and replaces the landform. Export heightmap downloads a ZIP bundle containing height.png and scale.json. Save scene keeps imported samples inside a portable .lithosky file.
  • Customize surface to blend sand, ground, rock and snow by height and slope, or select a single material preset. Color detail adds seeded variation. Surface changes support Undo and are saved with the scene. Use geological surface adds fine rock strata, fractures, soil/snow variation, bump detail and roughness to rendered stills; Feature scale controls their size.
  • Use studio lighting adds bounced terrain light, rough dielectric reflections, exposure and a filmic highlight curve. Add up to four rectangular lights and edit their position, direction, size, radiance and color. Preview remains approximate; render a still to see these lights. Save scene keeps these choices, and Undo restores earlier lighting.
  • For your first still: choose a scene, orbit until the mountain silhouette feels right, set the water level, then choose Render image. Save scene keeps the composition; Export PNG keeps the picture.
  • Open Graphics & compatibility to select a preview backend or retry after a graphics failure.

Open and Save scene use .lithosky files. Procedural scenes remain JSON; imported terrain uses a portable asset container. Autosave keeps local recovery snapshots between launches. Use Undo/Redo (Ctrl/⌘ Z and Ctrl/⌘ Shift Z) for scene and camera edits. Saving a scene downloads a new file; it does not update an earlier download. Use Render image for a progressive still up to 2048 pixels on its long edge with traced terrain, sun/sky lighting and calm-water reflection/refraction. Cancel keeps the last complete sample. Export PNG downloads the displayed image in sRGB. Changes to the landscape clear the render. Scene names do not. Build with layers creates a new terrain with rolling hills, ridges, domain warp and terraces. Reorder layers to change their effect; use Undo to return to your original scene. Generation runs in the background and can be canceled. Generation grids reach 2048 × 2048 cells; the preview stays at 128 × 128. Terrain detail selects 128², 512² or 1024² final geometry, capped by source resolution. Final rendering requires WebGPU; WebGL 2 remains available for preview. New scenes use geology, studio lighting, physical atmosphere and lake optics. Opened older files keep their settings; Upgrade rendering enables these together in one undoable edit and preserves sunlight direction. Use physical atmosphere for a computed Earth sky and aerial perspective in stills. Preview retains approximate haze. Lake optics adds absorption, refraction, filtered ripples and damp shores. Large waves and vegetation come later.
