Your first still

  1. Choose a scene. Use “Start with a scene” or the study cards. Alpine quiet starts with natural colors; A distant world starts with an imagined palette. A study replaces the composition; Undo restores the previous one.
  2. Find the silhouette. Drag horizontally to orbit around the terrain, then vertically to change your viewing height. Scroll to move closer or farther away.
  3. Shape the mood. Adjust Mountain height, Water level and Sun elevation. Try a lower sun to reveal terrain shadows.
  4. Render a still. Start at 640 × 360 and eight samples. Let the render complete, then choose Export PNG.
  5. Keep the composition. Save scene downloads the settings as a .lithosky file. File actions stay visible while the desktop workspace scrolls. Reopen it later to explore another direction.

Build a new landform

Choose Build with layers to compose rolling hills, ridges, domain warp and terraces. Add, reorder or disable up to eight layers. Warp bends the coordinates for layers below it; terraces shape the heights above them. Undo returns to the original scene.

Generation runs in the background and can be canceled or retried. Choose 256 × 256 through 2048 × 2048 cells. Preview stays lightweight at 128 × 128. Choose Detailed (512²) or Fine (1024²) under Terrain detail to resolve smaller ridges in final stills, capped by the source resolution. Save scene preserves the layer stack. New scenes retain their current v9 rendering models; legacy v1 scenes still open unchanged.

Layered terrain also supports Raise, Lower, Smooth and Flatten. Drag on land and release to apply one undoable stroke. Arrow keys position the brush; Space applies it; Escape cancels an unfinished stroke. Flatten uses the starting terrain height. Choose Orbit camera to turn the view again.

Files use the earliest format supporting every enabled capability: v2–v8 for legacy layer/sculpt/surface/import/sky/geology/studio features, or v9 with lake optics. Each scene holds up to 32 strokes of 24 marks each. Clear sculpting is undoable. History keeps at most 100 snapshots within a 4 MiB budget, dropping the oldest undo entries as it fills.

Use geological surface for finer rock strata, angular fractures, soil/snow variation and bump detail in final stills. Set Feature scale, strata, fractures, bump and roughness under Terrain surface. Preview shows approximate filtered colors; use Render image to judge the fine surface. These definitions require v7 or later and keep their settings through terrain and sky edits.

Bring in a heightmap

Import heightmap accepts 16-bit grayscale PNG or a heightmap bundle. Supported square sizes are 129, 257, 513, 1025 and 2049 samples. For plain PNG, set Black height, White height and Metres per scene unit under PNG import scale. Bundles restore their own scale. Import replaces the landform; Undo brings it back. Sculpt and material tools work on imported terrain.

Export heightmap downloads a ZIP bundle with height.png and scale.json. Save scene keeps the samples inside a portable .lithosky file. Keep that portable file as your backup; recovery manifests reference assets in your local data directory. Preview stays at 128²; final terrain can reach 1024² when the imported source supports it.

Try our original Terraced basin: heightmap bundle, portable scene, or separate 16-bit PNG and scale metadata. It represents 640 metres across the 64-unit patch. The scale label does not resize the camera or water.

Give the terrain its surface

Choose Customize surface to blend sand, ground, rock and snow by height and slope. Set the snow and sand heights, the slope where rock appears, and transition softness. Snow settles on gentler slopes; steep rock stays exposed. Select Rock, Snow, Sand or Ground for a single base material.

Color detail adds seeded variation; Detail frequency changes its scale. It changes color without moving the surface. Both Natural earth and Otherworld palettes work with these controls. Undo/Redo, files and recovery retain surface settings. Customized procedural surfaces save as v4; imported terrain uses v5, v6 with atmosphere, or v7 with geology. Older scenes keep their appearance until customized.

Follow the sun

Choose Use physical atmosphere, then render a still for an Earth sky and aerial perspective. Set elevation and azimuth manually, or choose Location & UTC date and enter latitude, longitude, year, day of year and minutes after midnight. North is −Z and east is +X. No location permission or network lookup is used.

Atmosphere metres per scene unit controls optical distance independently of heightmap metadata. Terrain origin altitude, aerosol density and ozone amount tune the atmosphere. The preview follows the sun direction but retains approximate haze. This first model uses single scattering; it does not include multiple scattering, clouds, stars or the moon.

Atmosphere requires v6 or later and preserve settings with terrain edits, Undo and recovery. Use artistic sky returns to the older appearance. Try the original Daylight basin scene.

Shape the light

Choose Use studio lighting for terrain interreflection, rough dielectric reflection, exposure and a filmic highlight curve. Exposure +1 doubles scene light before display. Sun and sky strength are independent. One to four terrain vertices trade transport depth for render cost.

Add up to four named rectangular area lights. Edit their position, direction, width, height, emitted radiance and linear RGB. Negative direction elevation points down. Render to inspect these lights; preview remains approximate. Lighting requires v8 or later and supports Undo, recovery and portable heightmap files.

Try Radiant highlands and compare the original lighting studies.

Give the lake depth

Lake optics adds refracted illumination, absorption along incoming and viewing paths, light-dependent scattering, terrain reflections, filtered normal ripples and damp shores. Optical density controls how quickly the bed disappears with depth. Water metres per scene unit sets optical scale; it does not resize geometry. Ripple strength and wavelength change reflections without displacing the saved shoreline. Zero strength produces calm water.

New scenes enable this model; older files can use Upgrade rendering or Use lake optics. Edits support Undo, files and recovery. The model is bounded single scattering with no caustics, underwater interreflection or refracted area-light illumination of the bed. Download Silver basin and see the water studies.

Move the camera

Set focal length, frame, roll and aim target under Camera & composition. Shift-drag or Shift-arrow keys pan; Escape cancels a drag. Save landscape, portrait or square framing with the scene. Depth of field traces an ideal circular aperture; Pick focus in view chooses terrain or water. Aperture affects blur, while lighting EV controls exposure. A 36 mm long-edge virtual sensor is cropped to the frame. Landscape-scale scenes usually remain sharp.

Arrange the scene

Open Objects beside the view or switch the left library to Objects. On small screens, Objects opens a dialog. Select terrain, water, sun/sky, the camera, area lights or groups in the named list. Search by name or type; arrow keys navigate the list. Select in view picks the nearest visible terrain, water or light, and the sky behind them. Escape in the view returns to orbit. The active camera is selected in the list.

Inspect shows the selected object’s controls. Rename it, choose a parent group, hide it or lock it. New group organizes existing objects. Group visibility and locks apply to descendants; select and unlock the parent before changing locked children. Hidden objects keep their component settings. Core scene objects can be hidden; area lights and groups can be deleted. Deleting a group keeps its children. Existing area lights can be duplicated, up to four.

Selection, tool mode and helper outlines are workspace settings. They do not dirty the document or enter the exported image. The first authored object edit upgrades an older file to v11 in one Undo step. Component data, stable IDs and groups survive save/open, portable heightmap containers and recovery. Existing terrain generation preserves unrelated entities. Move/rotate/scale handles, primitives and multiple editor cameras are planned for following slices.

Save & recover

Save scene downloads a new named file; it does not update a previous download. Open… restores a validated v1–v11 scene. Invalid files leave the current scene unchanged. Undo and Redo work for scene, camera and parameter edits.

Use Ctrl/⌘ S to save, Ctrl/⌘ O to open, Ctrl/⌘ Z to undo and Ctrl/⌘ Shift Z to redo. Local autosave keeps recovery snapshots between launches. At startup, restore a snapshot or keep the current scene to begin autosaving.

Wait for “Recovery saved on this device” before closing. Unacknowledged edits can be lost in a crash. A conflicting tab pauses autosave visibly; save a named scene before resolving the conflict.

Render & export

Choose 640–2048 pixels on the long edge and 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 or 256 samples per pixel. More samples reduce noise and improve antialiasing. A new complete image appears after each sample.

Cancel keeps the last complete sample for export. Canceling before the first sample leaves no image. Changing the landscape clears the render; renaming it keeps the image. After visual edits, render again to update the result. A completed render can be compared with the preview using Show preview / Show render.

Render a region selects a crop by dragging in the view or entering percentages. It keeps the full frame’s lens, material footprint and sampling. Export PNG saves only that crop. Region and image size are temporary settings; the camera lens and frame are saved.

PNG export contains the displayed image in 8-bit sRGB, without the interface. Resolution and sample count appear in the filename. Render settings and accumulated images are not saved in scene files.

Capabilities & limits

This build traces a bounded seeded heightfield, sun/sky lighting with terrain shadows, and a calm water plane with reflection and refraction. Optional studio lighting adds bounded terrain interreflection, rough dielectric surfaces, rectangular lights, exposure and filmic color. Natural and otherworld palettes are artistic choices. New scenes use physical atmosphere, geological surfaces, studio lighting and lake optics. Old files retain their saved models; Upgrade rendering enables missing models together in one undoable action and preserves the actual sunlight direction.

WebGPU live lighting preview uses the same rendering kernels within a 320-pixel long edge with 128² terrain after you pause editing. During gestures and in WebGL, the immediate raster preview remains approximate. Final geometry and sample counts can still change shorelines, silhouettes and noise. Final rendering requires WebGPU. Images may vary slightly across graphics devices and browsers.

Clouds, weather, vegetation, erosion, multiple atmospheric scattering, large displaced waves, animation, denoising and exports beyond 2K are not included yet. The current output is an early rendering foundation, with no photorealistic-quality guarantee.

The application uses no account, telemetry or remote rendering service. The installed browser remains separate software. Original code and generated content are Apache-2.0; the Go runtime has its own bundled license notice in distribution archives.
